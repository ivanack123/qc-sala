# QC SALA INSTALLATION GUIDE

App version: 1.0.2. Guide written in October 2026.

QC Sala is an unofficial app for playing at QuentinC’s Playroom (qcsalon.net) with your screen reader. It isn’t on the Google Play Store: you install it from a file. This guide walks you through it step by step, from the moment you receive the file until you’re in and playing, and tells you what you’ll hear on each screen.

### How to read this guide:

- Each part has a number and a title in capital letters. You can jump from one to another with your screen reader’s search.
- Each step is a single action.
- When the guide says "tap", with your screen reader that means: move to that item and double-tap.
- Text in quotation marks is what the screen says.
- Android shows its screens in your phone’s language. This guide uses Android’s names in US English. On other brands, such as Samsung or Motorola, they may be slightly different.

An honest note: this guide is new and hasn’t yet been tested from start to finish on a real phone. It has been tested on virtual phones with Android 10, 13, 14, 15 and 16, as Google ships them, opening the file from the Files app. Samsung, WhatsApp and Chrome haven’t been checked yet. If something doesn’t match what you hear, let the person who sent you the app know, so the guide can be corrected.


## 1. WHAT YOU NEED

1. A phone with Android 7 or later. It works on both 32-bit and 64-bit phones.
2. For direct touch, Android 11 or later. Direct touch lets you play by swiping one finger anywhere on the playroom screen (part 8). With Android 7 to 10 you can still play: you use the playroom with your screen reader’s usual navigation, the two- and three-finger gestures, and the buttons.
3. The app file: QC-Sala-1.0.2.apk. It’s about 4.7 MB. Files ending in .apk are Android apps.
4. The PIN, pattern or password you use to unlock your phone, if you have one. Android asks for it in some steps.
5. Your qcsalon.net username and password. If you don’t have an account, the app has a button to create one (part 6).
6. An internet connection, over Wi-Fi or mobile data.
7. About 10 minutes.
8. If possible, someone who can see the screen to help you, in case a step gets tricky. It’s not required.

If you don’t know which version of Android your phone has, look it up in the phone’s settings, under "About phone".

Only install the file you got from the person who shared the app with you, not one you find somewhere else.


## 2. SAMSUNG ONLY: TURN OFF AUTO BLOCKER

If your phone isn’t a Samsung, skip this part.

Many recent Samsung phones come with "Auto Blocker" turned on out of the box. While it’s on, the phone only lets you install apps from the Play Store and the Galaxy Store, so it won’t let you install QC Sala. That’s why you need to turn it off before opening the file.

1. Open your phone’s Settings.
2. Tap "Security and privacy".
3. Tap "Auto Blocker".
4. Turn off its switch. If it asks you to confirm or asks for your PIN, go ahead.
5. Tap Back to leave that screen.

If you can’t find "Auto Blocker", your phone most likely doesn’t have it: continue with part 3.

Once you’ve finished installing, you can turn it back on if you want. To install a new version of QC Sala by hand, you’ll have to turn it off again.


## 3. OPEN THE FILE

There are two ways: option A, if you got the file on WhatsApp, and option B, if you got a link.

### Option A, on WhatsApp:

1. Open the chat where you got the file.
2. Find the message with the file "QC-Sala-1.0.2.apk".
3. Tap it. If it has to download first, wait for it to finish and tap it again.
4. Android shows you a message about installing unknown apps. Continue with part 4.

If WhatsApp won’t open the file, ask the person who sent it for a download link and use option B.

### Option B, with a link:

1. Tap the link. Your browser downloads the file "QC-Sala-1.0.2.apk".
2. If your browser asks whether you really want to download it, confirm. Only do this if the link came from someone you trust.
3. When the download finishes, open your phone’s file manager app. On Samsung phones it’s called "My Files"; on other phones, "Files" or something similar.
4. Go into the "Downloads" folder. On some phones that folder opens by itself.
5. Tap the file "QC-Sala-1.0.2.apk".
6. Android shows you a message about installing unknown apps. Continue with part 4.


## 4. LET THAT APP INSTALL APPS

The first time, Android won’t let you install apps that don’t come from an app store. It asks you to give permission to the app you opened the file from: WhatsApp, your browser or your file manager. If you gave that permission before, Android skips this part and takes you straight to part 5. If your phone has Android 7, go further down, to "On Android 7 it’s different".

On some phones, for example with Android 10 or 13, when you open the file from "Files" you don’t get that message, but one with no title that says: "Your phone and personal data are more vulnerable to attack by unknown apps. By installing this app, you agree that you are responsible for any damage to your phone or loss of data that may result from its use." It has the buttons "Cancel" and "Continue". Tap "Continue" and go on to part 5: there’s nothing else to do in this part.

1. You’ll hear a message. Its title is usually the name of that app, but it may be an odd name, such as "Download Manager" or "Sounds"; that’s normal, ignore it. You can recognize it by its text: "For your security, your phone currently isn’t allowed to install unknown apps from this source. You can change this in Settings." It has the buttons "Cancel" and "Settings".
2. Tap "Settings".
3. The "Install unknown apps" screen opens. You’ll hear text saying that your phone and personal data are more vulnerable to attack by unknown apps. This is Android’s standard warning.
4. Turn on the "Allow from this source" switch. On some phones the wording may be slightly different.
5. Android goes back to the installation by itself. If it doesn’t, tap Back.

On Android 7 it’s different: there’s just one permission for the whole phone, and it’s called "Unknown sources".

1. Open your phone’s settings.
2. Tap "Security".
3. Turn on "Unknown sources". If it asks you to confirm, confirm.
4. Tap Back to leave the settings.
5. Open the file again (part 3) and continue with part 5.

Once you’ve finished installing, you can turn "Unknown sources" off.

On Android 8 or later, if you ever want to take this permission away from the app you installed from, you’ll find it in your phone’s settings: Apps, "Special app access", "Install unknown apps". On Samsung: "Security and privacy", "More security settings", "Install unknown apps". If you take it away, Android will ask you again when you install a new version.


## 5. INSTALL AND GET PAST PLAY PROTECT

1. You’ll hear "QC Sala" and "Do you want to install this app?", with the buttons "Cancel" and "Install".
2. Tap "Install".
3. You’ll probably get a "Google Play Protect" message, which is Google’s protection. You’ll hear "App scan recommended" and "Play Protect hasn’t seen this app before. To protect your device and data, send this app to Google for a security scan." Below that are "More details" and the buttons "Scan app" and "Don’t install app". It shows up because Play Protect doesn’t know the app, since it doesn’t come from the store. If you don’t get this message, skip to step 8.
4. Tap "More details". Do not tap "Don’t install app".
5. The message expands and now also says: "The scan may take a little while. You’ll see results on this screen. How Play Protect works". At the end of that text is "Install without scanning".
6. Tap "Install without scanning". Watch out: it isn’t a separate button, but a link inside that same text. If double-tapping does nothing, or something else opens, tap Back and look for the links in your screen reader’s menu. You can also ask someone to tap those words for you.
7. If your phone has a screen lock, you’ll hear "Install app without scanning?" and "Play Protect hasn’t verified this app". Enter your PIN, pattern or password. If it has no screen lock, the app installs without asking.
8. Wait. It can take up to a minute, while the screen says it’s installing and shows a single "Cancel" button: don’t tap it. When it’s done, you’ll hear "App installed.", with the buttons "Done" and "Open".
9. Tap "Open" and continue with part 6.

In step 4, instead of "More details", you can also tap "Scan app": the phone sends the app to Google to be checked, and the result appears on that same screen. That route hasn’t been tested yet.

If you tapped "Don’t install app", you’ll hear "App not installed.", with the "Done" button. Tap "Done" and open the file again (part 3). The permission from part 4 is already in place.

**If Play Protect blocks the app.** On some phones, instead of the message above, Play Protect might say "App blocked to protect your device" and not let you install it. This can happen because QC Sala includes an accessibility service, direct touch. If it happens to you:

1. Close the message.
2. Let the person who sent you the file know, so you can look for a solution together.
3. If you decide to install it anyway, you can pause Play Protect just while you install. Open the Play Store.
4. Tap your profile icon, at the top right.
5. Tap "Play Protect".
6. Tap the "Settings" button (a gear, at the top right).
7. Turn off "Scan apps with Play Protect". If it asks you to confirm, confirm.
8. Open the file again (part 3) and install QC Sala.
9. As soon as it’s done, go back to that same Play Store screen and turn "Scan apps with Play Protect" back on.


## 6. OPEN QC SALA AND SIGN IN WITH YOUR ACCOUNT

1. If you didn’t tap "Open", find the "QC Sala" icon among your apps and open it.
2. You’ll hear "QC Sala" and "Unofficial app for QuentinC’s Playroom". This is the sign-in screen.
3. Find the "Username" field and type your qcsalon.net username.
4. Find the "Password" field and type your password.
5. Leave the "Remember my details (to sign in again without typing them)" checkbox checked. It’s already checked.
6. Tap the "Sign in" button. You’ll hear "Signing in… Please wait."
7. Wait. On Android 13 or later, the first time, the "Playroom notifications" dialog appears first: continue with part 7. On Android 12 or earlier, you go straight into the playroom.

From then on, with "Remember my details" checked, when you open the app you’ll hear "Checking your saved qcsalon.net session, please wait." and you’ll get in without typing anything.

If you don’t have an account: below the "Sign in" button, under the "More options" heading, there’s the "Create an account (opens in your browser)" button. Right there you’ll also find "Forgot your password? (opens in your browser)".

If you hear "Couldn’t sign in: the username or password is incorrect, or qcsalon.net didn’t accept the sign-in.", check your username, which stays filled in, and type your password again.

The app doesn’t save your password.


## 7. NOTIFICATIONS

Notifications let your screen reader read you what the playroom says when the screen is off or you’re in another app, for example while you’re answering a message. Without them, you won’t hear it: it’s only kept in the history.

### On Android 13 or later, the first time you sign in:

1. A dialog called "Playroom notifications" appears. You’ll hear: "When you press “Continue”, Android will ask whether QC Sala can show notifications.", followed by what they’re for.
2. Tap "Continue".
3. Android asks whether QC Sala can send you notifications. Tap "Allow".
4. You enter the playroom.

On Android 12 or earlier, you won’t get this question.

If you told Android no, you can fix it later. As long as the permission is missing, the playroom offers you the "Playroom notifications" dialog again, with the buttons "Allow notifications" and "Not now". It only appears in the main room, not at tables, at most once a day, and it stops appearing once you’ve said no three times, counting Android’s "Don’t allow" when you first sign in. You’ll also find it in QC Sala’s Settings, in the "Screen reader and gestures" section, with the "Allow notifications" button. If Android no longer lets the app ask you, that button opens QC Sala’s notification settings: turn on the notifications switch there, then tap Back to return.

With notifications allowed, while you’re connected you’ll find the persistent notification "Connected to the playroom" among your notifications. Its "Disconnect" button takes you out of the playroom; you have to tap it twice, so you don’t leave by accident.


## 8. TURN ON DIRECT TOUCH

Direct touch is a small QC Sala accessibility service. It doesn’t read the screen or speak: it only lets the playroom screen receive your touches while it’s open. With it, you play with one finger: swipe up and down to move through the cards and options, left or right to change area, and double-tap to play, without changing anything about your screen reader. The Android navigation bar and your other apps are still handled by your screen reader. You only turn it on once.

If your phone has Android 10 or earlier, skip this part and parts 9 and 10: direct touch doesn’t appear there. In QC Sala’s Settings you’ll hear "Direct touch: requires Android 11 or later.", and you play with your screen reader and the buttons.

1. Enter the playroom with your screen reader on. After a moment, a dialog called "Direct touch" appears. You’ll hear: "To play by swiping one finger on the screen, turn on direct touch. You only do this once, in three steps:", followed by the steps. It has the buttons "Turn on direct touch", "More info" and "Not now".
2. Tap "Turn on direct touch". Android’s accessibility settings open. Sometimes the service’s page opens directly: in that case, skip to step 6.
3. Find "QC Sala: direct touch" in the list. It may be in the "Downloaded apps" section; on Samsung phones, in "Installed apps".
4. Listen to what your screen reader says along with the name. If you hear "Controlled by Restricted Setting", or it looks grayed out, go to part 9 now. If not, continue with step 5. Hearing "Off" is normal: it just means it isn’t on yet.
5. Tap "QC Sala: direct touch". Its page opens. On Android 13 and 14, instead of its page you get the "Restricted setting" warning, even though the service wasn’t grayed out: if so, go to part 9, step 3.
6. Turn on the first switch, "Use QC Sala: direct touch". Further down there’s another switch, the shortcut one: leave it off. If the switch is grayed out, or instead of the question in step 7 you hear "App was denied access" or "Restricted setting", go to part 9, step 3.
7. Android asks: "Allow QC Sala: direct touch to have full control of your device?", with the buttons "Allow", "Deny" and "Uninstall". It’s the usual warning for accessibility services: this one doesn’t read the screen, doesn’t speak and doesn’t store anything. Tap "Allow". Do not tap "Uninstall".
8. If it asks you about a shortcut (the accessibility button or the volume keys), don’t select any: you don’t need one, and it would clash with your screen reader’s shortcuts. If it won’t let you continue without choosing one, pick any of them, then turn off the shortcut switch on that same page.
9. QC Sala comes back by itself. You’ll hear: "Direct touch is on. In the playroom you no longer explore by touch: swipe one finger up or down to move through items, and double-tap to choose. “More options” is at the bottom left, and “Chat” at the bottom right." If QC Sala doesn’t come back by itself, tap Back until you get to it.

If your phone is set to a different language from QC Sala’s, the dialog in step 1 warns you: "Note: Android shows its settings in your phone’s language, not in QC Sala’s, so these names appear there in that language." If the service has a different name in that language, the dialog also tells you how it’s listed.

If you tapped "Not now", the dialog appears again the next few times you open QC Sala and enter the playroom, up to 3 times in total. You can also find it anytime in QC Sala’s Settings. To get there from the playroom, tap the "More" button (or "More options", if direct touch is already on) and then "App settings". On the sign-in screen, the "Settings" button is at the end. In Settings, in the first section, "Screen reader and gestures", there’s the "Turn on direct touch" button.

To turn it off, use the "Turn off direct touch" button in QC Sala’s Settings and answer "Yes". Don’t turn it off with a shortcut while the playroom is open: your screen reader may lose almost the whole screen, even in other apps. If that happens, press the same shortcut again, or restart your screen reader with its shortcut.


Recommended final step: keep it connected with the screen off. Android, especially on Samsung, may put the app to sleep with the screen off to save battery and cut your connection to the playroom. To prevent that, open QC Sala’s Settings and, in the first section, "Screen reader and gestures", below the notifications item, tap the button "Let QC Sala stay connected with the screen off". Android asks "Let app always run in background?": tap "Allow". Then Settings says "With the screen off, QC Sala stays connected to the playroom."


## 9. IF IT’S GRAYED OUT OR YOU GET A WARNING: ALLOW RESTRICTED SETTINGS

This usually happens on Android 13 or later when the app doesn’t come from an app store. Android asks for one more permission, which you only give once. Do it in this order: the option in step 7 doesn’t exist until Android shows you the warning in step 2.

1. In the accessibility settings, tap "QC Sala: direct touch" (or its switch, if you’re already on its page), even if it’s grayed out or your screen reader announces it as disabled.
2. Android shows you the "App was denied access" warning. You’ll hear: "Access to this permission can put your personal and financial info at risk." and "It’s possible the app won’t work properly without this restricted permission." On Android 13 and 14 the warning is called "Restricted setting", says "For your security, this setting is currently unavailable." and has the buttons "Learn more" and "OK".
3. Tap "OK". On Android 16 that button is called "Close". You don’t need to tap "Learn more" or the "Learn how to allow access" link: this guide tells you how.
4. Tap Back until you’re back in QC Sala. The "Direct touch is still off" dialog appears. You’ll hear: "It looks like direct touch didn’t turn on.", followed by these same steps, explained. It has the buttons "Open app info", "Back to accessibility settings" and "Close".
5. Tap "Open app info". Android’s "App info" screen for QC Sala opens. It has buttons such as "Uninstall" and "Force stop": don’t tap them.
6. Find that screen’s "More options" button (the three dots, at the top right) and tap it. If it isn’t there, open that same screen from your phone’s settings, as described in part 10, step 3: that way the button does appear. When you’re done, open QC Sala from your apps.
7. In the menu, tap "Allow restricted settings".
8. If your phone has a screen lock, you’ll hear "Verify it’s you" and "Enter your screen lock to continue". Enter your PIN, pattern or password. You may hear "Restricted settings allowed for QC Sala". If you don’t have a screen lock, it’s allowed right away.
9. Tap Back to return to QC Sala. The "Direct touch is still off" dialog appears again.
10. Tap "Back to accessibility settings".
11. Find "QC Sala: direct touch" again. It’s no longer grayed out. On Android 15, along with the name you’ll hear "Off".
12. Continue with part 8, from step 5: tap it, turn on "Use QC Sala: direct touch" and tap "Allow".

When you update QC Sala, you almost never have to do this again, but it can happen (see part 11). If you uninstall it and install it again, you do have to.


## 10. IF THE OPTION DOESN’T APPEAR

If "Allow restricted settings" isn’t in the three-dot menu, or you can’t find the three-dot button:

1. Check the order. The option only appears after the "App was denied access" (or "Restricted setting") warning. Before that warning, on Android 15 the three-dot button isn’t even there. Go back to part 9 and start from step 1: tap the grayed-out service until the warning appears.
2. If the "Direct touch is still off" dialog doesn’t appear when you go back to QC Sala (it doesn’t if more than 10 minutes have passed since you opened the settings), go to QC Sala’s Settings and tap "Turn on direct touch". In the dialog that appears, tap "Turn on direct touch" again. When Android’s settings open, tap Back: when you return to QC Sala, the "Direct touch is still off" dialog appears.
3. You can also get to the app info without going through QC Sala: in your phone’s settings, go to Apps, find "QC Sala" and open it. On some phones you first have to tap "See all apps", which usually includes the number of apps, as in "See all 26 apps". Also use this route if you opened app info from QC Sala and couldn’t find the three dots: in a test on Android 13 that happened, and this route showed them.
4. If your phone has Android 12 or earlier, this permission doesn’t exist. If the service won’t turn on, the problem is something else: let the person who sent you the app know.
5. If the option still doesn’t appear after all this, let the person who sent you the app know.

In the meantime, QC Sala works without direct touch: you can still use the playroom with your screen reader’s usual navigation and the buttons.


## 11. UPDATE TO A NEW VERSION

A new version of QC Sala always installs over the one you have. Don’t uninstall the old one: uninstalling deletes your settings, and you’d have to turn on direct touch and give the permissions all over again.

Since version 1.0.2, the app tells you by itself when there’s a new version and updates with its own button. Once a day it asks GitHub, the site where it’s published, whether there is one; the only things sent in that request are the app’s name and version number. The manual way, with the file, still works, and it’s the one you need if your version is older than 1.0.2.

### With the app’s button (since version 1.0.2):

1. When there’s a new version, a dialog called "A new version of QC Sala is available" appears. You’ll hear the new version number and the one you have, how big the download is and what’s new. Tap "Update". With "Not now", the dialog closes and doesn’t come back for that version until the next day.
2. The first time, Android needs you to let QC Sala install apps. A dialog called "One permission is missing" appears. Tap "Open settings", turn on the switch for QC Sala and go back to the app: the download starts on its own.
3. Wait. The app says "Downloading the new version" and tells you at 25, 50 and 75 percent. Then it checks that the file really is QC Sala.
4. Android’s installer opens. You’ll hear "Do you want to update this app?", with the buttons "Cancel" and "Update". Tap "Update" (on Android 10, "Install").
5. If Play Protect shows up, do the same as in part 5: "More details", "Install without scanning", and your PIN if it asks for it.
6. When it’s done, Android closes QC Sala. Open it again from its icon.

If on a Samsung the installation doesn’t move forward, turn off Auto Blocker (part 2) and try again. If you want to check yourself, without waiting for the notice, open QC Sala’s Settings and, under "Updates", tap "Check for updates now".

### By hand, with the file:

1. If you have a Samsung and you turned Auto Blocker back on, turn it off (part 2).
2. Open the new file the same way as the first time (part 3).
3. You’ll hear "Do you want to update this app?", with the buttons "Cancel" and "Update". On Android 10 you’ll hear "Do you want to install an update to this existing application? Your existing data will not be lost.", with the buttons "Cancel" and "Install". Before this, the message from part 4 may appear again: do the same as the first time.
4. Tap "Update" (on Android 10, "Install").
5. If Play Protect shows up, do the same as in part 5: "More details", "Install without scanning", and your PIN if it asks for it.
6. When it’s done, open QC Sala.

Normally everything is kept: your settings, your saved session, direct touch (it stays on) and the restricted settings permission. With the app’s button, everything is always kept, even on Android 14. Only if you update by hand, with the file, on Android 14, direct touch may be off: Android turns it off for any app updated from a file; it’s not a problem with QC Sala. When you enter the playroom, QC Sala tells you if this happened. To check yourself, open QC Sala’s Settings and listen to the direct touch line. If it says direct touch is off, tap "Turn on direct touch" and repeat parts 8 and 9.

If, while updating, the phone says the app wasn’t installed, don’t uninstall anything: let the person who sent you the file know.


## 12. IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

Nothing happens when you tap the file, or WhatsApp won’t open it. Ask for a download link and use option B in part 3.

You have a Samsung and it won’t let you install, even though you gave the permission in part 4. Turn off Auto Blocker (part 2) and open the file again.

You heard "App not installed." This happens, for example, if you tapped "Don’t install app". Tap "Done" and open the file again. If it happens again without you tapping "Don’t install app", let the person who sent you the file know.

Play Protect says "App blocked to protect your device". See the end of part 5.

**You can’t find the QC Sala icon.** Look for it with your phone’s app search. If it doesn’t show up, the installation most likely didn’t finish: open the file again.

You hear "There’s no internet connection, or qcsalon.net couldn’t be found." Check your Wi-Fi or mobile data and try again.

You can’t find "QC Sala: direct touch" in the accessibility settings. Look for it under "Downloaded apps" or, on Samsung, under "Installed apps". If your phone is set to a different language from QC Sala’s, the service may have a different name: QC Sala’s "Direct touch" dialog tells you which one. On Android 10 or earlier it doesn’t appear, because direct touch requires Android 11 or later.

Direct touch is on, but your one-finger gestures do nothing in the playroom. In QC Sala’s Settings, make sure the "Playroom gestures" checkbox is checked. It’s checked by default.

You turned off direct touch with a shortcut and your screen reader barely reads the screen. Press the same shortcut again, or restart your screen reader with its shortcut. Then turn direct touch off from QC Sala’s Settings.

**After updating by hand, direct touch is off.** This can happen. Repeat parts 8 and 9. With the app’s button it doesn’t happen.

**The app announces a new version, but it doesn’t install.** Read what the app says: it tells you whether the connection failed, the file is damaged, it isn’t QC Sala, or there isn’t enough space, and in all those cases it installs nothing. Check your connection and search again in Settings, "Updates", "Check for updates now". If nothing works, install the new file by hand (parts 3 to 5).

You don’t hear the playroom with the screen off or while you’re in another app. Notifications are missing: see part 7.

The person who sent you the app asks you for a log to track down a problem. In QC Sala’s Settings, near the end, under the "Debug log" heading, check the "Keep a debug log" box, repeat whatever went wrong and follow that person’s instructions. Don’t uncheck the box until they tell you to: unchecking it, or signing out, deletes the file. Note: while it’s on, it also saves the playroom chat and other players’ messages; it doesn’t save your password. Turn it on only when you’re asked to.


## 13. UNINSTALL

1. In QC Sala, open Settings: in the playroom, tap "More" or "More options" and then "App settings"; on the sign-in screen, tap the "Settings" button, at the end.
2. If direct touch is on, tap "Turn off direct touch". You’ll hear "Turn off direct touch? To turn it back on, you’ll have to go through Android’s accessibility settings again." Tap "Yes".
3. Leave QC Sala with your phone’s Home button.
4. Open your phone’s settings and go to Apps.
5. Find "QC Sala" and open it. On some phones you first have to tap "See all apps".
6. Tap "Uninstall". Don’t tap "Archive": that option removes the app but keeps its data.
7. Confirm that you want to uninstall it.

Your settings and your saved session are deleted from the phone. Your qcsalon.net account isn’t deleted.

If you want to leave your phone the way it was, take away the permission to install unknown apps from the app you installed from (end of part 4) and, on Samsung, turn Auto Blocker back on (part 2).

To learn how to play with QC Sala, read the [EASY GUIDE](easy-guide.md).
