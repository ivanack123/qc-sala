# QC SALA EASY GUIDE

For version 1.0.3 of the app.

This guide is short and simple. Each part has a number and a title. The steps tell you what to do and, often, what you’ll hear.

### A few words used in this guide:

- **Screen reader**: the program that talks on your phone, like Jieshuo or TalkBack.
- **Double-tap**: tap the screen twice in a row, quickly.
- **Swipe**: put your finger on the screen and move it to one side, as if you were brushing away a crumb.
- **Explore**: slide your finger slowly over the screen, without lifting it, so your screen reader says what’s underneath.
- **Edge sound**: a short sound that tells you there’s nothing more in that direction, or that what you did has no effect.


## 1. BEFORE YOU START: INSTALLING THE APP

If QC Sala isn’t on your phone yet, you need to install it first. The app comes in a file called QC-Sala-1.0.3.apk.

Another guide, the Installation Guide, explains step by step how to install it: it’s the file [INSTALLATION GUIDE](installation-guide.md). If you find it hard, ask someone you trust to help you with that part.

If you already have the app, go on to part 2.


## 2. WHAT QC SALA IS

QC Sala is an app for playing at QuentinC’s Playroom, online, with other people. You can play Uno, dominoes, poker and lots of other games.

It wasn’t made by the owner of the playroom. It’s a separate app, designed to be easy to use with a screen reader.

The app doesn’t have a voice of its own. Everything is spoken by your screen reader, in the voice you always use.

The app uses your phone’s language: if your phone is in English, the app is in English; if it’s in Spanish or French, the app is in that language. If your phone is in any other language, the app uses English. By default, what the playroom says also comes in your phone’s language, if the playroom has it; if not, in English. This guide uses the English names.


## 3. WHAT YOU NEED

1. A phone with Android 7 or later. Direct touch (part 6) needs Android 11 or later.
2. Internet (Wi-Fi or mobile data).
3. Your playroom username and password (qcsalon.net). If you don’t have an account, further down on the app’s sign-in screen there’s a "Create an account" button, which opens the playroom’s page in your browser. If you find it hard, ask for help creating one.


## 4. OPENING THE APP

1. Find the icon called "QC Sala" among your apps.
2. Open it the way you open any app. With your screen reader, that’s a double-tap.


## 5. SIGNING IN TO THE PLAYROOM

### The first time:

1. Find the "Username" field and type your username.
2. Find the "Password" field and type your password.
3. Leave the "Remember my details (to sign in again without typing them)" checkbox checked. It’s already checked.
4. Tap the "Sign in" button.
5. Wait. If your phone has Android 13 or later, a dialog called "Playroom notifications" appears. It explains what notifications are for: they let your screen reader read you what the playroom says even when the screen is off or you’re in another app. Tap "Continue". If that dialog didn’t appear, skip to step 7.
6. After the dialog, the phone asks whether QC Sala can send you notifications. Tap "Allow".
7. You’re in the playroom.

### After the first time:

1. Open the app.
2. Wait a moment. If you left "Remember my details" checked, you go straight into the playroom, without typing anything. You’ll hear "Checking your saved qcsalon.net session, please wait." and then "Session verified. Entering the playroom."

If you said no in step 6, then from the next day on, while you’re in the main room, another "Playroom notifications" dialog may appear, with two buttons: "Allow notifications" and "Not now". It appears at most once a day, and never at a table, so it doesn’t get in your way during a game. Once you’ve said no three times, counting step 6, it stops appearing. If the phone no longer lets the app ask, "Allow notifications" opens QC Sala’s notification settings: turn on the notifications switch there and come back with the Back button. You can also find it anytime in "App settings", under "Screen reader and gestures", with the "Allow notifications" button.

The sign-in sounds are the same as in the Windows program: when you open the app, you hear the playroom’s welcome sound; while it connects, a waiting sound; and when you get in, the connection-ready sound. They follow the volume of the playroom sounds.

The first time you connect over Wi-Fi, the app downloads the playroom sounds to your phone (about 3.5 MB, just once), so they play instantly. On mobile data, it only does this if you allow it in Settings, with the "Download the playroom sounds on mobile data too" checkbox.

If you hear that the username or password is incorrect, check your username (it stays filled in) and type your password again: the app clears it after each attempt.

The app doesn’t save your password.


## 6. TURNING ON DIRECT TOUCH

You turn on direct touch in your phone’s accessibility settings, as if it were a permission. With it, the whole playroom screen responds to your fingers when you play. It doesn’t change anything about your screen reader. You only turn it on once.

It’s only available on phones with Android 11 or later. If yours is older, skip this part: you play with your screen reader and the big buttons (part 9).

If you can’t see the screen well or you find it hard, ask someone you trust to help you with these steps.

Your phone’s settings appear in your phone’s language. Here are the words Android 15 uses in English; on other phones, especially Samsung, they may be a little different.

1. Sign in to the playroom with your screen reader on. After a moment, a dialog called "Direct touch" appears, with three steps.
2. Tap "Turn on direct touch". Your phone’s accessibility settings open.
3. Find "QC Sala: direct touch". It may be under "Downloaded apps"; on Samsung phones, under "Installed apps". Tap it. If your screen reader says "Controlled by Restricted Setting" (the service is grayed out), read "If the service is grayed out" further down. If the phone opened the service’s page directly, go on to step 4.
4. Its page has two switches. Turn on only the first one, "Use QC Sala: direct touch". The other one is a shortcut (on Android 15 it says "QC Sala: direct touch shortcut"): leave it off.
5. The phone asks: "Allow QC Sala: direct touch to have full control of your device?" It’s the usual warning for accessibility services. Tap "Allow".
6. If it asks you about a "shortcut", don’t select any. If it won’t let you continue without choosing one, pick any of them, then turn off the shortcut switch on that same page.
7. That’s it: the app returns on its own to where you were. You don’t have to find your way back. If it doesn’t come back on your phone, press the Back button until you’re back in QC Sala.
8. You’ll hear "Direct touch is on." and how to play.

If you want to know more before turning it on, tap "More info" in the dialog.

### If the service is grayed out:

In step 3, the service may be grayed out and say "Controlled by Restricted Setting". This usually happens when the app doesn’t come from an app store: Android asks for one more permission. Do this:

1. Tap "QC Sala: direct touch" anyway. Android warns: "App was denied access" (on Android 13 and 14 it may say "Restricted setting"). Tap "OK".
2. Press the Back button until you’re back in QC Sala. The "Direct touch is still off" dialog appears.
3. Tap "Open app info".
4. Open Android’s "More options" menu (the three dots at the top right) and choose "Allow restricted settings". If asked, enter your PIN or screen lock. This option only appears after the warning in step 1.
5. Press the Back button until you’re back in QC Sala. The dialog appears again: tap "Back to accessibility settings" and continue from step 3 of the list above.

If that option doesn’t appear in the menu, ask the person who installed the app for help. In the meantime, play with your screen reader and the big buttons.

If you tapped "Not now" in the "Direct touch" dialog, that’s fine. The dialog appears again on a later visit to the playroom, once the app has been fully closed; after you tap "Not now" 3 times, it stops appearing. To turn it on whenever you like: tap "More", then "App settings", and in the first section, "Screen reader and gestures", tap the "Turn on direct touch" button. The same dialog appears: tap "Turn on direct touch" again.

With direct touch on, the whole playroom screen is for playing with your fingers. Your screen reader still handles the phone’s bottom bar (Back, Home and Recents) and two buttons in the bottom corners, "More options" and "Chat" (part 7).


## 7. HOW THE PLAYROOM SCREEN IS LAID OUT

### With direct touch on:

- The whole screen is the game area.
- There are two buttons in the bottom corners, just above the phone’s buttons (Back, Home and Recents):
  - **Bottom left**: "More options". Everything the big buttons do is there: leaving, settings, help and more (part 9).
  - **Bottom right**: "Chat". It’s for writing in the chat (part 10).
- To find them, put your finger right on that corner, without dragging it from the middle of the screen, and explore there until your screen reader says its name. Then double-tap right there, in the corner: a double-tap anywhere else on the screen goes to the game and would play the card or option you’re on.

Without direct touch, your cards are at the top and, further down, the history, the chat and the big buttons. There are no corner buttons.

### The playroom has two parts for playing, called areas:

- **Game**: your cards.
- **Game options**: the game’s options, such as passing your turn. Only when the game has options. On the screen they’re shown on separate lines, so people with some sight can read them in full.

If a game uses numbers or letters (dice, for example), the "Game keyboard" is at the end of Game options. Double-tap to open it, swipe one finger up or down to hear each button, and double-tap the one you want. Back takes you back.

The playroom messages (the history) aren’t an area: you hear them with two fingers, as part 8 explains.


## 8. THE BASIC GESTURES

These gestures work with direct touch on. Do them anywhere on the playroom screen, except on top of the two buttons in the bottom corners.

1. One-finger swipe up: you hear the previous card.
2. One-finger swipe down: you hear the next card.
3. One-finger swipe right: you move from Game to Game options. If the game has no options, you just hear the edge sound. On a board (like chess), one-finger swipes left and right first move column by column; at the last column you hear the edge sound and, if you keep going, you move to Game options.
4. One-finger swipe left: you go back to Game.
5. One-finger tap: it repeats the card or option you’re on. Handy if you get lost.
6. One-finger double-tap: you play the card you heard, or choose the option you heard.
7. Two-finger swipe down: you draw a card, in games where you draw.
8. Three-finger tap: in almost every game, you hear whose turn it is. It answers as soon as you lift your fingers.
9. Three-finger hold (keep three fingers still for a moment): you hear who’s at the table, without lifting your fingers. It works in every game except the ones you play alone (Solitaire and 2048), where it tells you the most useful thing for that game.
10. The playroom messages are like a line: the oldest on the left and the newest on the right. Two-finger swipe left: you go back. The first time, you hear the playroom’s latest message; if you do it again, you hear the earlier ones, one by one. Two-finger swipe right: you go forward, toward the newest. While you’re reviewing, new messages that arrive don’t move you from where you are.
11. You can rest a finger on the screen while you think: nothing happens. If you then swipe, the swipe counts.
12. Two-finger hold: keep two fingers still for a moment and lift them (the phone vibrates gently when you can). "Game gestures" opens, with everything you can do in that game and what each gesture does.
13. Two strokes in a row with one finger, without lifting it: down then left is Back, like the phone’s Back button. Down then right, done twice, opens your screen reader’s menu. The first time you hear “Your screen reader has the screen: repeat the gesture”; the second time your screen reader does it.

The first time you join each game, you hear: "Hold two fingers on the screen to learn this game’s gestures." That’s gesture 12.

When you play or draw, you hear everything the playroom says in a single announcement. The card you end up on isn’t announced: you hear it when you move through your cards or with a one-finger tap. If you want to hear it, go to "App settings" and, under "Screen and motion", check "Announce the card you end up on after playing or drawing".

### In that same section there are two more checkboxes, which are off by default:

- **"Announce the card you draw"**: when you draw, you also hear which card you got, at the end of what the playroom says. It works in games where you draw from the pile, like Uno, and in domino games.
- "When you draw, announce that you drew instead of repeating the total": this is mainly for Ninety nine. By default, when you draw in that game, you hear the total, just like in the Windows program. With the box checked, you hear that you drew a card.

If you use Jieshuo: Jieshuo cuts off an announcement when another one arrives. So if a new announcement comes in while the previous one is still being spoken, the app first tells you what was left and then the new one. Sometimes you’ll hear the end of a sentence again; that’s on purpose, so you don’t miss any move. If you make a gesture, the new announcement cuts in, just like on the computer. If it repeats too much, ask for help: in "App settings", under "Advanced", you can change "Your speech rate in Jieshuo".

In the app, Chess, Connect four, Go and Reversi can only be played against people: they won’t start with bots. If you add one, you hear the playroom’s message and then: "In this app, this game can only be played against people: remove the bots and invite someone to the table." In all the other games you can play with bots.

### In Uno:

- To say "Uno!", shake the phone. You can also swipe up with two fingers.
- To declare a bluff on whoever played the +4, swipe up with three fingers. It only works if the table is using the Bluff rule.
- If the phone counts a shake you didn’t mean, go to "App settings" and, under "Screen and motion", tap "Shake sensitivity" and choose "Firm": that way only a strong shake counts. If it’s hard to get a shake to count, choose "Gentle".

### In Monopoly:

- To pay to get out of jail, swipe up with three fingers.
- To bid in an auction, swipe down with three fingers.

The app figures out on its own which game you joined. You don’t have to choose anything. If you ever hear "Nothing until I know the game", wait a moment and try again. If nothing changes, ask for help: in "App settings", under "Advanced", you can choose it with the "Choose the game manually" button.

When a gesture doesn’t do anything in that game, you hear the edge sound and "Nothing in this game". Nothing bad happens.

If you ask for something, like whose turn it is, and the playroom doesn’t answer, after a moment you also hear the edge sound. The first time at each table, you hear why, in a sentence that starts with "The playroom didn’t respond".

Some risky moves, like throwing away a card for good, ask you first. For example, you hear: "Fold? To do it, repeat the gesture; otherwise, do nothing." To make the move, repeat the same gesture within 5 seconds. If you do anything else, it’s canceled and you hear "Canceled."

To find out which gesture does what in the game you’re playing: open "More options", move to the "Table and playroom" group and choose "Quick game help" (part 9). It tells you each thing with its gesture, for example: "Two-finger tap: see the current pile total." To go back to the game, tap "Back to the game", at the end, or use the Back button.


## 9. THE BIG BUTTONS

Without direct touch, the big buttons are at the bottom. They do the same things as the gestures:

- **"Play or confirm"**: plays the card you’re on.
- **"Draw"**: draws a card. In other games its name changes and tells you what it does. It only appears once the app knows which game you joined.
- **"Context menu"**: asks the game for the menu of the card or option you’re on, in games that have one.
- **"Game gestures"**: opens a list of everything you can do in that game. For example, "Whose turn it is".
- **"Back"**: goes back in the game menu.
- **"More"**: opens more options, like "App settings", "Gesture help" and "Leave the playroom".

With very large text on the phone, or with little room on the screen (for example, with the phone turned sideways, in landscape), only "Play or confirm", "Back" and "More" stay at the bottom. The other buttons move into "More".

Without direct touch, play as usual with your screen reader: move through your cards and double-tap the one you want to play. You can also use these buttons.

With direct touch, all of that is in "More options", the button in the bottom-left corner:

1. Put your finger right on the bottom-left corner, just above the phone’s buttons, without dragging it from the middle, until you hear "More options". Double-tap right there, in the corner.
2. More options fills the whole screen and always opens on its first option. At a table, you hear: "More options. Gameplay. Leave the table". Away from a table: "More options. Gameplay. Connection status", and how the connection is doing.
3. Swipe down with one finger to hear the next option, and up for the previous one.
4. The options are in four groups: Gameplay; Table and playroom; History; and Help, settings and exit. Swipe right with one finger to move to the next group, and left to go back.
5. When you hear the one you want, double-tap.
6. To leave More options, use the phone’s Back button. You return to where you were.

The gestures for playing, like drawing with a two-finger swipe down, also work inside More options.


## 10. WRITING IN THE CHAT

### With direct touch:

1. Put your finger right on the bottom-right corner, just above the phone’s buttons, without dragging it from the middle, until you hear "Chat". Double-tap right there, in the corner.
2. The keyboard opens. Type your message.
3. Tap the keyboard’s send key, or the "Send" button.
4. To go back to the game, use the phone’s Back button. You return to where you were.

### Without direct touch:

1. Find the chat field, at the bottom, and double-tap it.
2. The keyboard opens. Type your message.
3. Tap the keyboard’s send key, or the "Send" button.

The field’s name tells you who will read your message: at a table, "Message to your table"; in the main room, "Message to the whole playroom (everyone can read it)".

To resend a message you already wrote: in "More options" (or in "More"), in the "Table and playroom" group, choose "Reuse a message you sent" and then the message.


## 11. LEAVING THE TABLE AND THE PLAYROOM

### To leave the table (and go back to the main room):

1. With direct touch, open "More options", the button in the bottom-left corner. Without direct touch, tap the "More" button.
2. At a table, the first option is "Leave the table". Double-tap it.
3. You’re asked just once whether you’re sure:
   - If the game hasn’t started yet, it’s almost always the app that asks, with a dialog: "Are you sure you want to leave the table?" The default button is "No". Tap "Yes". You hear "Leaving the table."
   - If the game has already started, the playroom asks: you hear "Leaving the table." and, right after, its question: "Are you sure you want to leave the table?" Its "Yes" and "No" appear in the Game area, where your cards were: move to "Yes" and double-tap.
4. While the playroom answers, a double-tap doesn’t play any card, in case you tap one too many times.

### To leave the playroom, with direct touch:

1. Open "More options" and swipe right with one finger until you hear the last group, "Help, settings and exit".
2. The first option in that group is "Leave the playroom". Double-tap it.

### To leave the playroom, without direct touch:

1. Tap the "More" button.
2. Find "Leave the playroom". It’s the last option in the dialog. Tap it.

Either way, it asks you: "Are you sure you want to leave the playroom? The app will close; you’ll stay signed in." The default button is "No". Tap "Yes". The app closes and you’re back where you were on your phone. The next time you open QC Sala, you go straight in, without typing your username or password.

In "More options", at the end of the "Help, settings and exit" group, far from "Leave the playroom", there’s "Sign out of qcsalon.net". Only use it if you want the phone to forget your account. That one does take you back to the sign-in screen.


## 12. IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

- **If you hear that there’s no internet**: check that Wi-Fi or mobile data is on and try again.
- **If the game got cut off**: wait a little. The app almost always reconnects on its own. You can also ask it to with "Reconnect", which only appears when the connection is down: in "More" it’s the first option; in "More options", it comes right after the connection status.
- If the app asks for your username and password again: type them in again.
- If you don’t hear the playroom with the screen off or while you’re in another app: allow notifications (part 5).
- If the playroom gets cut off when you turn the screen off: Android may put the app to sleep to save battery. In "App settings", tap the button "Let QC Sala stay connected with the screen off". Android asks "Let app always run in background?": tap "Allow".
- **If the gestures don’t do anything**: in "App settings", under "Screen reader and gestures", check that direct touch is on and that the "Playroom gestures" checkbox is checked. If nothing changes, turn off direct touch with the "Turn off direct touch" button and play with your screen reader and the big buttons.
- If you can’t find the "More options" and "Chat" buttons: put your finger right on one of the bottom corners of the screen, just above the phone’s buttons, without dragging it from the middle.
- **If you want to learn more**: tap "More" (or open "More options" and move to the "Help, settings and exit" group) and then "Gesture help". First you hear the basics; to learn everything, tap "Full help".


## 13. KEEPING THE APP UP TO DATE

Since version 1.0.2, QC Sala tells you by itself when there’s a new version. A dialog appears with two buttons: tap "Update", wait, and when Android asks "Do you want to update this app?", tap "Update" again. The first time, the app asks for one permission and takes you to the right setting: turn on the switch for QC Sala and go back. If you want to check yourself, in "App settings", under "Updates", tap "Check for updates now".


## 14. WHO TO ASK FOR HELP

- The person who installed the app for you.
- A family member or friend you trust who can see the screen.

An important tip: never tell anyone your password in the playroom chat.
